The ARCHER2 Service was a world-class advanced computing resource for UK researchers. For such researchers, there were specific allocation mechanisms for requesting ARCHER2 time via the ARCHER2 partner research councils EPSRC and NERC as detailed below. 

The [ARCHER2 User Support Team](mailto:support@archer2.ac.uk) offered advice and assistance to prospective users and could offer guidance as to what was the best route of access. [Further contact details can be found here](https://www.archer2.ac.uk/support-access/servicedesk.html).
The ARCHER2 CSE Team could also provide [assistance with completing a Technical Assessment (TA)](https://www.archer2.ac.uk/news/2022/08/03/technical-assessments.html "Help on completing a successful TA") and could also be contacted via the [Service Desk](mailto:support@archer2.ac.uk). 

This page has a number of sections describing the access mechanisms that were available:

[How to prepare a successful Technical Assessment](https://www.archer2.ac.uk/news/2022/08/03/technical-assessments.html) - a blog post with helpful advice on how to complete TA forms. **Note:** Technical Assessments were not required at application time for Access to HPC calls.

The [Understanding Package Performance course materials](https://www.archer2.ac.uk/training/courses/211028-package-performance/#materials) provided an introduction to producing and plotting application performance, which could help users complete the ARCHER2 technical assessment form - particularly "6.1 Quantitative evidence that the code scales efficiently". 

Users with questions about measuring application performance or scaling, or who needed assistance producing this information, could contact the [ARCHER2 Service Desk](mailto:support@archer2.ac.uk).

<a id="driving-test"></a>
### ARCHER2 Driving Test

A small allocation (800 CU) for new ARCHER2 users to get them started using the ARCHER2 Service.  Further details on the [Training Driving Test page](https://www.archer2.ac.uk/training/driving-test.html).  Included access to GPU nodes.

#### ARCHER2 GPU Driving Test

Similar to the original Driving Test but with additional questions on using the GPUs, this granted access to the GPU nodes (minimal CU allocation), and was open to those who were previously granted CUs via the Driving Test.  Further details on the [Training Driving Test page](https://www.archer2.ac.uk/training/driving-test.html#gpu-driving-test)


<a id="pump-priming"></a>
### ARCHER2 Pump Priming

EPSRC remit researchers could request a small amount of resource awarded through a light-touch process in order to try, test and scale their code on ARCHER2. This was the successor scheme to ARCHER Instant Access. Applicants could [complete the ARCHER2 Pump Priming TA form (docx)](ta/ARCHER2-PumpPriming-form.docx) and send to the [ARCHER2 Service Desk](mailto:support@archer2.ac.uk). The ARCHER2 CSE team would review the form and send to EPSRC for a decision on access. Decisions were typically made in less than 2 weeks (and usually much sooner). A maximum of 4000 CU for 6 months were available through Pump Priming.


<a id="pioneer-projects"></a>
### ARCHER2 Pioneer Projects
EPSRC remit researchers could apply for large amounts of ARCHER2 compute for ambitious, computationally intensive simulations and calculations in support of an individual project through EPSRC's ARCHER2 Pioneer Projects call.  Projects could be up to 2 years in length.  This was the successor scheme to the ARCHER Leadership Calls.

<a id="access-to-hpc"></a>
### Access to HPC
Provided an open and relatively flexible route to computational support for projects in EPSRC remit.

Researchers could apply for access to national HPC systems (both ARCHER2 and most of the Tier-2 services). This call combined what was previously the ARCHER RAP (Resource Allocation Panel) and the Tier-2 Open Access call. Calls were expected to open every six months.


<a id="topup"></a>
### ARCHER2 Top Up
Only available for existing EPSRC grant holders with an ARCHER allocation. Provided additional resource for users who already had an allocation from an EPSRC grant, but needed more to complete the objectives of their grant. Successor to the ARCHER Top Up process.

<a id="DirectorTime"></a>
### Director Time - University of Edinburgh Researchers
EPCC had a small allocation on ARCHER2 and encouraged applications from University of Edinburgh researchers. The resource was intended to assist researchers gaining access to ARCHER2 but was not meant as a replacement for applying for time via the EPSRC and NERC calls especially where larger amounts were required.  

<a id="consortia"></a>
### Access via the EPSRC High End Computing Consortia
If a researcher's work was within remit of one of the Scientific Consortia, formed around research communities with substantial and continuous computational needs, then they could apply for ARCHER2 time by joining the relevant consortium. Each consortium had its own way of applying for access and allocating time. The [ARCHER2 Scientific Consortia](https://www.archer2.ac.uk/research/consortia/) pages have more information on the remit of the individual consortia and their contact details.

<a id="epsrc-grants"></a>
### Access through EPSRC grants and fellowships
Researchers could include ARCHER2 on EPSRC funding applications when applying for funding (if they already had an
active grant then they should consider applying via one of the calls for ARCHER2 time only that were described
above).

When submitting proposals to EPSRC Standard Mode (Grant) that included ARCHER2 resources on the UKRI Funding Service, applicants should add ARCHER2  under Section 6: Facilities

- facility name: ARCHER2
- usage: the requested resource in CU as *unit* and leave cost as *blank*. Reasonable ARCHER2 allocations were awarded for the duration of the EPSRC funding. 
- confirmation of facility agreement: this asked for confirmation that a [Technical Assessment Form](ta/ARCHER2-TA-form.docx) had been completed and agreed with the ARCHER2 service desk. Applicants did not need to submit this TA as part of the application in TFS, but in the application they did need to confirm they already had it agreed. Should an application be funded then grant funding would not be released until EPSRC had sight of the relevant TA.  

EPSRC operated a 6-monthly allocation period policy for access on ARCHER2. The total amount of compute resource
(measured on ARCHER2 as *CU*) awarded was broken down into 6-month periods that span the length of access to ARCHER2
that was requested. Applicants specified the period profile on the TA form when they applied for access.
EPSRC period allocations were enforced on ARCHER2 in the following way: 

- Any unused allocation at the end of an allocation period was lost  
- Users could not move CU between different allocation periods

For questions about accessing ARCHER2 via EPSRC, researchers could see
[the HPC pages on the EPSRC website](https://epsrc.ukri.org/research/facilities/hpc/) or
[contact the EPSRC Research Infrastructure team](mailto:researchinfrastructure@epsrc.ukri.org).


<a id="nerc"></a>
### NERC Remit
Applicants first contacted the relevant NERC science area leader and checked the
NERC policy on access. Full details were on the [NERC website](https://www.ukri.org/councils/nerc/facilities-and-resources/find-a-nerc-facility-or-resource/applying-to-use-high-performance-computing-services/).  Applicants within the NERC remit wishing to access ARCHER2 via this route contacted the relevant consortia leader for advice ahead of submission. Once authorisation was obtained from NERC and if submitting through the UKRI Funding Service portal, NERC applicants should add ARCHER2 as a facility in the portal.


<a id="ukri-calls"></a>
## UKRI calls, fellowships and responsive mode
For access as part of applications to other UKRI research councils, researchers could
[contact the EPSRC Research Infrastructure team](mailto:researchinfrastructure@epsrc.ukri.org) in the first
instance to discuss requirements and plans. 

ARCHER2 resources via EPSRC and NERC awards were included as notional costs rather than real costs. Resources for 
UKRI awards in other research areas may have needed to be included as real costs. 


<a id="other-calls"></a>
## Access via non-UKRI calls, fellowships and other routes

For access as part of applications from non-UKRI funded researchers, researchers could
[contact the EPSRC Research Infrastructure team](mailto:researchinfrastructure@epsrc.ukri.org) in the first instance to 
discuss requirements and plans. Please note that these discussions could take some time, and UKRI could not guarantee that an agreement would be reached.
