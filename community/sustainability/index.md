Environmental sustainability was a key focus for the ARCHER2 service—both in terms of day-to-day operation and research and development activities—to understand how the environmental sustainability of ARCHER2 could continue to be improved over its lifetime and to develop improvements for future services. This included work on understanding and communicating emissions from ARCHER2 within the [Greenhouse Gas (GHG) Protocol framework](https://ghgprotocol.org/) to support improving the carbon efficiency of the service and awareness among users; and working to ensure that biodiversity was promoted at the ACF data centre where ARCHER2 was housed.

## Emissions

The ARCHER2 service was committed to reducing its carbon footprint and
contributing to the University of Edinburgh's commitment to being zero carbon by
2040.

This section provides more information on the estimated greenhouse gas (GHG)
emissions from the ARCHER2 service.

### Impact on reducing emissions

As well as a producer of GHG emissions, HPC systems like ARCHER2 also contributed
to reducing emissions. The main source of reduced emissions from services such as
ARCHER2 was in the research that led to new technology, policies and approaches
to reducing emissions. Some examples included:

- HPC services run the climate models that are used to provide evidence for setting
  emissions reductions policies and targets across the world.
- Research and modelling on HPC services leads to development of improved zero
  emission energy generation by, for example, modelling new wind turbine and wind
  farm designs.
- Modelling to support the development of new energy storage technologies such as
  improved batteries.

The emissions reductions from such activities are extremely difficult to 
quantify for a number of reasons so, at the moment, these are not factored in
to the emissions estimates for ARCHER2.

As well as the research activities on the service leading to reductions in emissions,
there were other activities that HPC services could potentially take. For example:

- Using the waste heat generated by large scale HPC services as a heat source for
  homes, businesses or farming. For the ACF data centre where ARCHER2 was hosted,
  options were being investigated on how to do this.
- Incorporating environmental and biodiversity improvements into the service. For
  the ACF data centre (which was in a rural location) work was undertaken to
  improve the site biodiversity and improve habitats. Responsible carbon offset
  schemes could also potentially be used to reduce emissions if they were undertaken
  as part of the service. 

### Estimating emissions from ARCHER2

#### Scope 3 emissions

Scope 3 emissions from the ARCHER2 hardware were estimated from a subset of
the components that were expected to make up the majority of the emissions. Note that
there was a large amount of uncertainty for Scope 3 emissions due to lack of high quality
Scope 3 emissions data from vendors. In particular, the number used for the compute node
emissions was at the high end of estimated values and the actual value could be as much
as 15% lower at around 900 kgCO<sub>2</sub>e/node.

| Component | Count | Estimated kgCO<sub>2</sub>e per unit | Estimated kgCO<sub>2</sub>e | % Total Scope 3 | References |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|---|
| Compute nodes | 5,860 nodes | 1,100 | 6,400,000 | 84% | (1) |
| Interconnect switches | 768 switches | 280 | 150,000 | 2% | (2) |
| Lustre HDD | 19,759,200 GB | 0.02 | 400,000 | 6% | (3) |
| Lustre SSD | 1,900,800 GB | 0.16 | 300,000 | 4% | (3) |
| NFS HDD | 3,240,000 GB | 0.02 | 70,000 | 1% | (3) |
| Total | | | 7,320,000 | 100% | |

The per-CU (nodeh) Scope 3 emissions were estimated by assuming a service lifetime
of 6 years and 100% availability:

```
7,320,000 kgCO2e / (5,860 nodes * 6 years * 365 days * 24 hours) = 0.023 kgCO2e/CU
```

All the tools described below used a value of **0.023 kgCO<sub>2</sub>e/CU** for Scope 3
emissions from ARCHER2.

References:

1. [IRISCAST Final Report](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.7692451)
2. Estimate taken from IBM z16™ multi frame 24-port Ethernet Switch Product Carbon Footprint
3. [Tannu and Nair, 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.10793)

#### Scope 2 emissions

Scope 2 emissions from ARCHER2 were zero as the service was supplied by 100% certified renewable energy.
For information purposes the Scope 2 emissions that would have occurred if the energy
was not 100% renewable energy could be calculated using the methodology described below.

The ARCHER2 team was aware of ongoing discussion in the sustainability community about the impact and
effectiveness of certified renewable energy contracts that were supplied through UK National Grid
connections. These discussions were monitored and advice was taken from sustainability professionals
on how to report and estimate ARCHER2 emissions.

UK National Grid based Scope 2 emissions were calculated using the compute node energy use for particular
jobs along with the carbon intensity of the South Scotland region of the UK National Grid at the start
time of the job. The carbon intensity was retrieved from the [carbonintensity.org.uk](https://carbonintensity.org.uk/)
web API.

If the energy use of a job was not available (which happened occasionally due to, e.g. counter failures) then
the mean per node power draw from 1 Jan 2024 - 30 Jun 2024 on ARCHER2 was used to compute the energy
consumption. This corresponded to a value of 0.41 kW per node.

Estimates of power draw of individual components of ARCHER2 suggested that the compute node power draw made up
around 85% of the system power draw so 15% of the measured energy was also added to the 
value to estimate the contribution from other components.

| Component | Count | Loaded power draw per unit (kW)| Loaded power draw (kW) | % Total | Notes |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|---|
| Compute nodes | 5,860 nodes | 0.41 | 2,400 | 85% | Measured by on system counters |
| Interconnect switches | 768 switches | 0.24 | 240 | 9% | Measured by on system counters |
| Lustre storage | 5 file systems | 8 | 40 | 1% | Estimate from vendor |
| NFS storage | 4 file systems | 8 | 32 | 1% | Estimate from vendor |
| Coolant distribution units | 6 CDU | 16 | 96 | 3% | Estimate from vendor |
| Total | | | 2,808 | 99% | |

Overheads from the electrical and cooling plant varied with outside weather conditions at
the data centre but were typically less than 10%. As a conservative estimate in the tools 
developed, 10% of the total energy was added to get the estimated energy use including plant 
overheads.

The final process for calculating the energy use of a job was:

1. Retrieve the measured job energy from Slurm (or compute energy based on a node power draw of
   0.41 kW if the value was not available from Slurm).
2. Add 15% of this value to estimate energy use by other components.
3. Add 10% of the new value to estimate overheads from the plant.

## Biodiversity

The UK was ranked as the lowest G7 nation for biodiversity intactness and had seen a
19% decline in species abundance since 1970. The University of Edinburgh was a
signatory of the Nature Positive Universities Alliance and EPCC was committed to
understanding the impact service activities had on biodiversity. The team
investigated the impact on biodiversity from the production of ARCHER2 and looked at
ways to reduce the impact during decommissioning through effective waste management.
As mentioned in the emissions section above, a key aim was also to protect and enhance 
biodiversity on the ACF site. Firstly, work was undertaken to protect the existing
biodiversity on site, with the machine room built around a traditional central tree
roundel and an area of wetland left untouched for wildlife. Work was also undertaken to
enhance conditions for biodiversity to thrive, planting diverse native hedgerows,
creating wildlife meadows for pollinators and creating wildlife corridors through
fencing. Felled wood was used to provide habitats for insects on site.
